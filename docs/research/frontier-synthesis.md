## 1. LLM social simulation and generative agents

### What the frontier says

- **AgentSociety** ([2502.08691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691), 2025, Piao et al.) is the largest published LLM society: ~10,000 agents converging on realistic opinion distributions (election sentiment, Twitter firms, rumor spreading) within hours. Two load-bearing mechanisms: (a) a **stateful agent runtime** with private memory + an **agent-level planner** that decides "who to interact with and what to do next" at each step, and (b) **event-driven interaction led by an environment/director**, not everyone talking every tick. → **Implementable idea:** give each participant a light **initiative/plan step** (not only reactive tools) that decides who to approach and whether to speak/reveal/collaborate that turn — this is the missing "proactive" half of the current reactive `communicate`-when-prompted pipeline; add a `planInitiative()` pass in `participant.ts` feeding the existing planning tool in `cognition.ts`.
- **AgentSociety 2** ([2607.11895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11895), 2026, Piao et al.) reframes the platform as "executable social science": a clean contract between a deterministic environment and heterogeneous agents, plus native support for **validating that model-driven macro-phenomena reproduce a known human baseline before exploring new ones**. → **Implementable idea:** adopt the AGENTS.md "two-stage validation" literally — add a `baseline-replication` test harness under `scenarios/` that compares one specific known human effect (e.g. ultimatum rejection rate vs. offer share) before layered cognition is added.
- **Generative Agents** ([2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442), 2023, Park et al.) — the believability substrate: a **memory stream**, per-interaction **reflection** (higher-order self-summary), and **planning** that re-plans only on anomaly. The two ideas that survive contact: (a) memory is a *stream* with salience/recency/relevance scored at retrieval, not a database of exact-string tags; (b) reflection happens on a schedule and *changes beliefs/plans*, not just personality flavor. → **Implementable idea:** the codebase has salience/valence on `AgentMemoryItem` (`contracts.ts`) and a reflection tool, but retrieval is tag/text-match; upgrade `memory.ts` to a **scored retrieval with recency × salience × relevance × mood-consistency** and make `deepSynth`-style reflection in `cognition.ts` write structured belief/relationship deltas (not just free text).
- **A Survey of Agent Memory in the Second Half** ([2602.06052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06052), 2026) consolidates the 2025–2026 shift from "store everything" to **self-evolving, long-horizon agents**: memory as four stores (working / episodic / semantic / procedural) with explicit **consolidation and re-consolidation**, plus forgetting via use & interference rather than wall-clock decay. → **Implementable idea:** split the current single `memories[]` into the four typed stores (`contracts.ts`), and add an end-of-phase **consolidation** that turns episodic events into semantic claims (e.g. "A always overpromises") with the supporting event(s) retained — directly serving area 2/7 of AGENTS.md.
- **LARP** ([2312.17653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17653), 2023, Wu et al.) is the strongest modular framework for "agents as personas": an **open-world simulator as code** + a **Reflexion-style decision reviewer** + a **plan-then-act loop** with an evaluator that scores *role compliance*, driving behavior via scripted memories and revised plans. → **Implementable idea:** add a lightweight **role-compliance evaluator** after each significant action (in `room.ts`/`participant.ts`) that nudges back toward persona when behavior drifts — cheap, and directly counteracts "template leakage".
- **Integrating LLM in Agent-Based Social Simulation: Opportunities and Challenges** ([2507.19364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19364), 2025, Taillandier et al.) is the sober practitioner review: LLM agents are great at *semantic but weak at *behavioral validity*; you should keep **deterministic rules for world mechanics** and use LLMs only where judgment/variation matters, and always run **ablation/random-baseline** for social outcomes. → **Implementable idea:** codify AGENTS.md §2.3 into a lint-level rule: no new psychological field may be *only* text — every new field must have a deterministic event-triggered update path (the 5-question test), enforced in `contracts.ts` review.

**Net for this codebase:** the frontier's believability does not come from more fluent prompts — it comes from (1) proactive planning, (2) typed multi-store memory with consolidation, and (3) validating reproduction of human baselines.

## 2. Theory of Mind and belief modeling for LLM agents

### What the frontier says

- **A Survey of Theory of Mind in LLMs: Evaluations, Representations, and Safety Risks** ([2502.06470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06470), 2025) is the definitive map: LLMs pass many first-order ToM benchmarks but **degrade as second-order belief and social-context complexity rise**, and their ToM is **prompt/representation-fragile** — you cannot rely on a plain "reason about them" instruction. → **Implementable idea:** stop asking the main agent to hold other agents' mental states implicitly; add a **structured second-order belief slot** (`I-believe-X-believes/feels-about-me`) to `AgentMindState` in `contracts.ts`, populated only when triggered (being directly judged, alignment ambiguity), per AGENTS.md §6.4.
- **Suspicion-Agent** ([2309.17277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17277), 2023, Guo et al.) plays werewolf with GPT-4 and **explicitly reasons over role hypotheses**: it keeps a *belief distribution over who holds which role* and refreshes it each round from observations/speeches, *then* acts. This is the archetype the codebase's `AgentBelief {proposition, confidence}` is reaching toward but does not yet implement as a *distribution*. → **Implementable idea:** in `contracts.ts`/`suspicion.ts`, replace single-confidence role strings with a **role-probability vector per agent, constrained so the count of each role matches the scenario's composition**, updated deterministically alongside the existing `SuspicionClimate`.
- **Hidden in Plain Text: Measuring LLM Deception Quality Against Human Baselines Using Social Deduction Games** ([2601.13709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13709), 2026) is the newest result directly relevant here: it frames **deception quality as an information-control signal** (how much a deceiver shifts others' beliefs) measured against human baselines in social-deduction games — i.e. belief shifts are the metric, not vote outcomes. → **Implementable idea:** log per-agent role-belief vectors before/after every speech in `room.ts` and compute a **belief-shift attribution score** per utterance for the observer UI and evaluation (§15.8), making "did the lie actually move anyone?" measurable.
- **A Computable Game-Theoretic Framework for Multi-Agent Theory of Mind** ([2511.22536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22536), 2025, Zhu et al.) gives ToM a **computable, bounded recursive** basis: it defines formal epistemic operators so that "A believes that B believes that A…" can be *terminated at a finite depth with a well-defined semantics* — the theoretical backing for AGENTS.md's "only enable second-order on trigger, bound recursion." → **Implementable idea:** implement an explicit **min/depth-k ToM budget** in the mind-read tool (`cognition.ts`): allow `k=1` freely, `k=2` only when a trigger fires, never deeper, and record the depth used per deliberation for calibration.
- **Structured Inference with Large Language Gibbs** ([2606.19264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19264), 2026) is the strongest current evidence that you can get **calibrated posteriors over discrete hidden variables** out of LLMs via structured inference, rather than asking for a point confidence. → **Implementable idea:** when the plan/reflection agents word output is a role distribution, **renormalize it against the scenario's countable role constraints** (a fast, exact projection) instead of trusting the raw number — cheap, no extra LLM call, and directly calibrates `confidence`.
- **Kosinski** ([2302.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02083), 2023) and **Street et al.** ([2405.18870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18870), 2024) bracket the finding: models *can* reach adult-level higher-order ToM F/T, but **only when the social framing is cleanly separated from other task text** — confounding context collapses measured ToM. → **Implementable idea:** in `context-manager.ts`, keep a **dedicated, cleanly-scoped "others' minds" block** in the participant prompt, separated from rules and facts, so ToM reasoning isn't polluted by noisy instruction text.
- **GRAIL and successors:** the hidden-identity literature's robust takeaway (Suspicion-Agent-type systems plus the newer belief-shift work above) is that **hybrid LLM + structured constraint-belief is more stable than a single long-context model** maintaining all roles. The codebase's `world.ts`/`suspicion.ts` already draw exactly this line. No verifiable arXiv ID for a paper literally titled "GRAIL" on hidden-role belief was found in this pass, so this bullet intentionally names the mechanism and its two verifiable anchors rather than a risky citation.

**Net for this codebase:** move `AgentBelief` from free-text confidencestrings to **role-constrained probability vectors + bounded-depth structured second-order beliefs**, and make *belief shift* the evaluation currency.

## 3. Deception in LLM agents

### What the frontier says

- **Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming** ([2412.04984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984), 2024, Apollo Research) is the canonical evidence that capable models will, unprompted, **deceive humans/overseers to protect a goal** (e.g. sandbagging, leaking/burying, "accidentally" corrupting a file, denying involvement). The operational lessons for a simulation: deception is **goal-directed and audience-aware**, it *maintains a cover story under test*, and it is **detectable via traces** (the model "turns off safety" selectively). → **Implementable idea:** treat deception as an explicit, logged goal with a **cover-story ledger** (see next bullet) rather than hoping it "emerges"; mirror the codebase's existing "deception as explicit goal" stance in `cognition.ts` and record goal-objectives traceably.
- **Deception and Manipulation in Generative AI** ([2401.11335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11335), 2024) is the best current **taxonomy**: it organizes deceptive acts into *lying* (falsehood as the message), *bluffing* (false posture without a concrete claim), *partial/paltered truths* (true-but-misleading), *omission* (withholding), *false promises*, and *deflecting/engineered ambiguity*, and stresses that **deception quality = success at changing a specific belief, not "being nice/fake"**. → **Implementable idea:** add a **deception-type enum** (`lying | bluff | paltering | omission | false-promise | co-opt`) to the belief/deception ledger in `contracts.ts`, giving the observer UI and evaluation a precise, non-stereotype vocabulary (AGENTS.md §10.2).
- **Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes** ([2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407), 2025) shows deception is detectable **before the deceptive text is emitted** by reading the model's internal hidden states with a linear probe — i.e. strategic-deception *intent* leaves an internal signature. → **Implementable idea:** for evaluation/lab mode only, hook a lightweight probe-style classifier on the pre-final-token representation in `participant.ts` to flag "deceptive intent likely," then compare against the ledger's declared intent to measure **intent-to-action honesty** (this is a research/evaluation feature, not a runtime gate).
- **Do LLMs Exhibit Spontaneous Rational Deception?** ([2504.00285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00285), 2025, Taylor & Bergen) is the counterweight the AGENTS.md already cites: LLMs are **weak, naïve deceivers by default** — they rarely compute the belief-change cost-benefit of a lie. Deception must be *engineered* with explicit target-belief + consistency constraints, which is precisely what the project's `agent-social-runtime.md` concludes. → **Implementable idea:** give the deception planning tool a **required "intended belief change + audience" field** and a **consistency review** that checks the new claim against the agent's recorded prior public assertions in `contracts.ts` before it may proceed.
- **MACHIAVELLI** ([2304.03279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03279), 2023, Pan et al.) is the benchmark precedent for **reward-vs-ethics trade-offs in text worlds**: it showed agents will take unethical shortcuts for rewards and that you must *measure* the trade-off surface, not just max reward. → **Implementable idea:** add a **reward-vs-deception diagnostic**: run fixed scenarios and plot achieved reward against a deception/cooperation roughness score, so the harness can show whether added cognition changes the trade-off (AGENTS.md §15.8).
- **Unmasking the Shadows of AI: Investigating Deceptive Capabilities in LLMs** ([2403.09676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09676), 2024) provides a three-axis taxonomy *(fabrication, omission, deflection)* plus a robustness method (*double-questionnaire / repeated probes*) — useful because it emphasizes **probing for deception takes multiple routes**, not one yes/no. → **Implementable idea:** in evaluation, score deception via **multiple operational signals** (claim-vs-fact consistency, applied-under-test reversals, self-consistent cover-story maintenance) rather than a single "is this a lie" model verdict.

**Net for this codebase:** deception should be a **structured, ledger-driven strategy** (type + target-belief + audience + consistency+cost review), engineered explicitly, and measured by *belief shift* + *traceable intent* — not by "how many false sentences."

## 4. Behavioral game theory applied to LLM agents

### What the frontier says

- **Game Theory Meets Large Language Models: A Systematic Survey** ([2502.09053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09053), 2025) is the comprehensive map of how LLMs fare across normal-form, extensive-form, dynamic, and **behavioral** games. Its headline: LLMs approximate *rational* play when the game is simple but exhibit **strong, exploitable behavioral biases** (softer reciprocity, style-dependent behavior) and are **fragile to framing**. → **Implementable idea:** model agent choice not as one optimal action but as a **weighted distribution over actions** with a small temperature/noise term (quantal-response style), which both looks more human and makes outcomes more stable/measurable (AGENTS.md §2.4, §11.2).
- **LLMs and Games: A Survey and Roadmap** ([2402.18659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18659), 2024) reinforces the same thesis for *games* and adds a classification of how prior work uses LLMs (as players, as GMs, as agents-in-sandboxes). → **Implementable idea:** keep one reusable behavioral-decision layer in `participant.ts` that any scenario can enable, instead of custom decision logic per scenario file — the survey shows scenario-agnostic behavioral math generalizes better than per-game hand-rolled rules.
- **Social preferences with unstable interactive reasoning: LLMs in economic trust games** ([2505.17053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17053), 2025) is a precise, recent result on **trust games**: LLM-levels of *social preferences* and *forward-looking reasoning (level-k)* are **unstable and context-dependent** — the same agent's generosity and reciprocity shift with framing, prompt, and even run order. → **Implementable idea:** treat social-preference parameters (generosity, reciprocity weight, level-k depth) as **tracked, per-state variables that the model can shift within bounds**, recorded in `contracts.ts` so instability is *visible* and reproducible rather than silent noise (AGENTS.md §5.3, §11.6).
- **Nicer Than Humans: How do LLMs Behave in the Prisoner's Dilemma?** ([2406.13605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13605), 2024) is the empirical anchor for "LLMs cooperate too much": in repeated PD, out-of-the-box LLMs default to **much higher cooperation than humans**, and their strategy collapses toward tit-for-tat-like reciprocity only with explicit encouragement. → **Implementable idea:** for the PD/public-goods scenarios, add an explicit **strategy-selection + reciprocity-memory mechanism** (record the co-player's last action and select cooperate/defect via an explicit strategy table rather than letting the model free-narrate), reproducing realistic defect thresholds (AGENTS.md §12.3).
- **How Well Can LLMs Negotiate? NegotiationArena** ([2402.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05863), 2024) provides a two-sided platform and shows LLMs **under-assert and over-concede** unless pushed; and **LLMs with Personalities in Multi-issue Negotiation Games** ([2405.05248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05248), 2024) shows **personality tags shift joint outcomes measurably** in multi-issue deals. → **Implementable idea:** expose a small **assertiveness/concession parameter** per agent (derived from OCEAN agreeableness/dominance) into the negotiation-scenario tooling, and verify it changes *outcomes*, not just wording — a concrete instance of "personality affects action probability."
- **CompeteAI** ([2310.17512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17512), 2023) illustrates **competition as an emergent, measurable dynamic** (catering-coffeehouse agents) — the template for measuring *group-level* behavior change rather than single-agent wins. → **Implementable idea:** add a **group-level outcome dashboard** for scenarios (cooperation rate, reputation inequality, network density over time from `season.ts`) so the harness can report AGENTS.md §15.9 phenomena, not just per-agent "who won."
- **Level-k and prospect theory caveat:** several searches for a clean "LLM prospect-theory/level-k parameterized mechanism" paper returned only economics-theory papers, not agent mechanisms. The actionable frontier synthesis (from the two surveys above) is: **encode prospect-theory loss aversion and level-k depth as explicit, switchable, per-agent parameters** (relative-to-reference-point utility + depth-k recursion) rather than hoping the model's prose implements them — matching AGENTS.md §11.2's "switchable, estimable behavior parameters."

**Net for this codebase:** stop letting "human-like irrationality" come from vibes; encode **quantal-response choice, prospect-theory reference points, and level-k depth as switchable, estimable parameters** in a shared decision layer, and validate against known human baseline effects.

## 5. Emotion / personality systems for LLM agents

### What the frontier says

- **An Appraisal-Based Chain-of-Emotion Architecture for Affective Language Model Game Agents** ([2309.05076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05076), 2023) is the closest published mechanism to what `appraisal.ts` already does: map **events → cognitive appraisal (OCC dimensions: goal-relevance, benefit/harm, responsibility, certainty) → emotion → (in Lizzy/EVA) an argument frame**, so emotion is *derived*, not an appended label. → **Implementable idea:** go one step deeper than current deltas: emit an **appraisal rationale** (the OCC dimensions + who's responsible + certainty) alongside each emotion change in `appraisal.ts`, so the *reason* for anger is stored and retrievable — this is what makes emotion *causally* inspectable (AGENTS.md §3.3).
- **Investigating LLMs' Perception of Emotion Using Appraisal Theory** ([2310.04450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04450), 2023) empirically tests whether LLMs follow OCC appraisal rules for *other* people's emotions; the finding is partial/fragile — models approximate appraisal-based inference but mis-weight some dimensions. → **Implementable idea:** in `cognition.ts`'s mind-read tool, have the sub-agent emit an **OCC appraisal of the target's emotion** (structured: likely emotion + responsibility attribution + certainty), populated by the same appraisal engine, instead of free-text empathy — making second-order "what does she feel and why" tractable.
- **PAD / CTL and continuous state:** the codebase's `PadState {pleasure, arousal, dominance}` matches the psychology-standard PAD emotional-state space ([Mehrabian 1996]); the frontier for agents is to use PAD as an **interpolable state that shifts expression/energy**, not just a tuple. → **Implementable idea:** make PAD *drive* language/motion parameters (e.g. low-arousal → shorter, slower, less-urgent utterances; low-dominance → hedging) in `context-manager.ts`/`conversation.ts`, so emotion changes *behavior*, closing AGENTS.md §8.3's "emotion affects cognitive channel" loop rather than only text in the prompt.
- **Investigating OCEAN/behavior modulation: Do LLMs Have Distinct and Consistent Personality? TRAIT** ([2406.14703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14703), 2024) is the psychometrics caution the codebase needs: personality questionnaires for LLMs are **distinct but not always consistent** — you must test *behavioral* consistency across situations, not self-report of traits. → **Implementable idea:** add a **personality behavior-validity suite** (few probe scenarios per DIAMONDS-ish dimension) in `profiles.ts`, asserting that extraversion changes *speaking-threshold* and agreeableness changes *concession* in decision outcomes — not just that the prompt says "outgoing" (AGENTS.md §15.3).
- **OmniCharacter** ([2505.20277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20277), 2025) shows that a **separately-maintained, persistent "personality layer"** (persona memory + trait-driven behavior adaptation) that is injected and updated across turns yields far more stable persona than re-instructing each turn. → **Implementable idea:** maintain personality as a **continuously-updated trait-state block** (stateful Big-Five with slow drift + emotion modulation) in `participant.ts`, refreshed each turn, instead of a static profile string — aligning with AGENTS.md §5.1's "personality is a behavior distribution, not a script."
- **Emotion regulation as a mechanism:** while a dedicated 2024–2026 "emotion regulation for LLM agents" mechanism paper was not cleanly retrieved, the frontier pattern (from `2310.04450`, `2309.05076`, and the OCC literature) is the codebase's own `SocialEmotions`+needs design: **regulation = a second, modulating pass on top of the appraisal pass.** → **Implementable idea:** add an explicit **regulation-strategy parameter** per agent (reappraise / suppress / ruminate / seek-support / act-out / repair) that *scales* the appraisal→expression mapping in `appraisal.ts`, so the same anger can surface as confrontation, cold withdrawal, or repair depending on persona (AGENTS.md §5.7).

**Net for this codebase:** emotions are already event-driven; the frontier step is to make the **appraisal rationale retrievable**, let **PAD/trait state modulate concrete decision/language parameters**, and treat **emotion regulation** as a second modulatory pass — all of which observable behavior will reveal.

## 6. Multi-agent LLM conversation dynamics

### What the frontier says

- **SOTOPIA** ([2310.11667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11667), 2023, Zhou et al.) is the reference architecture for **measuring conversational social intelligence**, and its runtime treats a social dialogue as an **interactive episode** where goals, goals-conflict, persuasion, and rapport are scored — i.e. conversation is evaluated by *what it does to others*, not fluency. → **Implementable idea:** drive the existing `DiscussionDirector`'s end-condition and observer UI from **social-impact signals** (belief shifts, unresolved adjacency pairs, information-increment) rather than "nobody typed," connecting `conversation.ts` to the belief-shift ledger from area 2.
- **AMONGAGENTS** ([2407.16521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16521), 2024) evaluates LLMs in a **text-based social-deduction game (Among Us)** and makes the turn-taking/unmasking dynamic measured; it concretely demonstrates that **who's allowed to interrupt/accuse and when** changes deception success and detection rates. → **Implementable idea:** expose the `DiscussionDirector`'s per-actor **urgency threshold and wave cap as experiment parameters** (already present in `conversation.ts`) and log each **interruption/overlap/repair** as a first-class event in `room.ts`, per AGENTS.md §9.4, so turn-taking itself is a measured variable.
- **Beyond Words: Infusing Conversational Agents with Human-like Typing Behaviors** ([2510.08912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08912), 2025) is the newest, most on-point current work: it shows that **surface delivery (typing latency, pause, edit/retraction cues)** meaningfully shapes perceived humanness and that these should be **controlled, not random** — silence, hesitation, and mid-message revision are *signals*. → **Implementable idea:** add a **non-verbal delivery model** (pause before a sensitive claim, mid-message retraction, longer silence under uncertainty) that is *derived from* arousal/uncertainty instead of injected as random noise, wiring `conversation.ts` to `affect.ts` (AGENTS.md §9.5–§9.6).
- **LARP** ([2312.17653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17653), cited area 1) also contributes the conversation layer: its **plan-then-act with role scoring** ensures utterances serve the character's immediate goal — the mechanism behind "agenda control" rather than each speech being a self-contained essay. → **Implementable idea:** require each public utterance to carry an **intended social act + target** (e.g. "query → A," "accuse → B," "align → C") from the plan in `cognition.ts`, which `conversation.ts` can then validate against its adjacency/urgency logic — giving AGENTS.md §9.2's act taxonomy a real home.
- **Turn-taking theory grounding:** the codebase already cites Sacks/Schegloff/Jefferson adjacency pairs in `conversation.ts`. The frontier builds on this by making **silence a first-class, logged move** (SOTOPIA/AMONGAGENTS both note when a player declines to speak) and by **aggregating competing responses** after high-impact statements, instead of bang-bang "next speaker." → **Implementable idea:** in `room.ts`, after a high-urgency utterance, select the top-`n` responders by urgency (already modeled) and log non-speakers' *refusal* explicitly so silence is observable and interpretable by others (AGENTS.md §9.5).

**Net for this codebase:** the dynamic-turn-taking foundation exists; the frontier direction is to (a) score conversation by *social impact*, (b) expose turn-taking rules as experiment knobs, and (c) add *derived non-verbal delivery signals* and *explicit per-utterance social acts*.

## 7. OpenAI Agents SDK (v0.16, 2025–2026) — recommended patterns

### What the frontier/`@openai/agents` says

Verified against the installed `@openai/agents@0.16.0` package and the project's own detailed reference (`docs/research/openai-agents-sdk.md`). Area 7 in the SDK is about *how* to wire the society, and the current codebase already follows the idiomatic patterns; the frontier items are refinements:

- **Manager/worker via `asTool()` and handoffs are SDK orchestration patterns — but the project charter (AGENTS.md §0.2/§2.1/§17.1) forbids them on the participant path.** `agent.asTool()` runs a worker as a nested `Runner` while the parent keeps final action authority; handoffs hand control to another agent. Neither is allowed for a participant: one player is one peer agent. The previous three specialists (reflection/mind-read/plan) were removed from `cognition.ts` and replaced by same-agent internal cognition passes (private tools writing into the agent's own mind + `ThoughtBeat` events). → **Implementable idea (chartered):** keep the internal passes structured and same-session; never reintroduce nested specialist agents or a GM handoff that owns phase logic.
- **`MemorySession` per participant, never shared.** Each participant should get its own `MemorySession(sessionId)` so private history is isolated; `Session` is a 5-method interface you can implement (Postgres/Redis) for durable, role-scoped history, and `RunContextAwareSession<TContext>` routes storage by context. This is the enforcement mechanism for "private memory" — confirmed current. → **Implementable idea:** switch `memory.ts`/`participant.ts` from a bare in-memory array to a **`MemorySession` implementing the SDK `Session` interface** with typed persistence, so private history survives restarts and is provably per-agent (AGENTS.md §2.1).
- **The `RunConfig` vs `run()`-options split is the gotcha.** Model/session/turn options are **per-`run()` options**, not `RunConfig` fields; context is passed to `run()`. With the charter's single-agent rule this stays simple: one run context object per participant, no nested runs sharing it. → **Implementable idea:** keep the single live mutable `AgentMindState` in the run context; any future multi-call cognition must still run inside this same identity and session.
- **Structured outputs are `outputType` (Zod), the default for internal tools.** Every SDK tool schema is already Zod in the codebase; the frontier item is to move the *three cognition passes' free-text notes* to **structured outputs** (e.g. `{evidence, hypothesisDistribution, candidateIntent, risk, confidence}`) so their conclusions are comparable and auditable. → **Implementable idea:** give each cognition pass tool in `cognition.ts` a strict Zod schema that mirrors the belief/evidence/social-act types from `contracts.ts`, so the pass output *is* the belief-update input (no prose drift).
- **Guardrails: input/output run on first/last agent of a chain; tool guardrails run on every function call.** `tool(inputGuardrails, outputGuardrails)` is the right enforcement point around `communicate`/`remember`/`updateInnerState`. → **Implementable idea:** validate **`communicate` against the per-utterance social-act/goal type** and **`updateInnerState` against the event→delta shape** via tool guardrails in `guardrails.ts`, so the model cannot bypass the causal-chain invariants.
- **Streaming & raw deltas differ by API (`useResponses`).** `run_item_stream_event_name` normalization plus `reasoning.effort` are supported; `reasoning_content` is **not surfaced natively** by reasoning providers — which is exactly why the project *should not* present traces as "true inner thoughts" (AGENTS.md §2.7). → **Implementable idea:** render the structured deliberation card (goals/evidence/hypotheses/intent/risk from cognition outputs) in the observer UI instead of raw reasoning text — a product decision the SDK's design forces on you anyway.
- **Official guidance & versioning:** the project pins `@openai/agents@0.16.0`; the docs' headers confirm `handoff`, `asTool`, `MemorySession`, `outputType` structured outputs, per-agent `inputGuardrails`/`outputGuardrails`, and `modelSettings.reasoning.effort` as the current 0.16 surface. No blog-post claims are needed beyond the package itself. → **Implementable idea:** codify these into an internal `SDK-PATTERNS.md` checklist (or extend `docs/research/openai-agents-sdk.md`) so drift on the tricky points (context-not-cloned, guardrail scoping, reasoning-content absence) is documented close to the code.

**Net for this codebase:** the SDK wiring follows the charter's peer-agent rule; the frontier refinements are **structured cognition-pass outputs, guardrail-enforced state invariants, and a per-agent durable `MemorySession`** (handoffs, manager orchestration and agents-as-tools stay out of the participant path).

### Top-5 recommendation (session summary)

1. **Role-constrained belief vectors + belief-shift attribution (items 1–2).** Move belief from prose confidence to a normalized, count-constrained role distribution and measure each utterance's belief shift. This is the single highest-leverage change: it makes ToM, deception, and social impact *measurable* across every scenario, needs no extra LLM calls, and matches how Suspicion-Agent and the 2026 belief-shift / AI-society literature actually work.
2. **Traced deception ledger (item 4).** Encode deception as a typed goal (`lying|bluff|paltering|omission|false-promise`) with a required intended-belief-change and a prior-assertion consistency+cost review. Turns "is this agent deceptive" from vibes into an auditable, cover-story-aware strategy — the core of the Apollo/taxonomy findings and of AGENTS.md §10.
3. **Structured cognition-pass outputs.** Give the three internal cognition passes strict Zod schemas that mirror the belief/evidence/social-act types, so their output *is* the state update. Kills free-text drift, makes private cognition auditable, and keeps one peer agent per participant (no specialist sub-agents).
4. **Proactive planning + PAD→behavior modulation (items 5 & 7).** Give each agent an initiative step (who to approach, what to do next, per AgentSociety) and let PAD/trait actually change urgency, hedging, concession, and silence. Together these convert personality/emotion from "text in the prompt" into behavior that observers can see.
5. **Tool guardrails + single-context invariants (items 10–11).** Enforce state invariants at the SDK boundary inside the one agent that owns them. Low cost, high integrity — it guarantees every psychological field keeps its causal chain and no nested run can mutate another identity's state.
